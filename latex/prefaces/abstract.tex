  \noindent{\textbf{Abstract}}\\
\end{center}

This Master's Thesis develops an end-to-end solution for estimating and explaining implied volatility in IBEX options. Starting from real trades, contracts, underlying futures and EONIA/\texteuro{}STR rate series, the project builds an implied-volatility database through financial validations and numerical inversion of the Black-76 pricing model. Several regression families are then trained, including linear regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, neural networks and a classical quantum-inspired architecture.

The main contribution is not limited to predictive modelling. The project turns trained models into explainable and queryable artefacts through a dashboard and a FastAPI service. Explainability is addressed through global and local SHAP, volatility surfaces, ICE and ALE curves, surrogate trees, symbolic regression, historical neighbours and residual diagnostics. This combination supports the assessment of accuracy, robustness, financial coherence and local support for individual predictions.

The methodology uses temporal splits, shared-contract controls, temporal k-folds and composite metrics that penalise instability and overfitting. The results chapter is prepared to include final metrics, compare standard training against progressive ATM training, select the best-performing model and analyse the dashboard views in detail. The resulting architecture is reproducible and oriented towards model validation, internal audit and operational use in financial environments.
